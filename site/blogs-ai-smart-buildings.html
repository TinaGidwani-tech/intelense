Your Building Is Wasting Energy Right Now. AI Can Fix That.

Meera IyerSmart Spaces Lead, IntelenseApril 20263 min read

Buildings account for 40% of global energy consumption. Most of that energy is wasted — on heating empty rooms, running HVAC systems on fixed schedules regardless of occupancy, and maintaining equipment past its optimal service window. The systems managing most commercial buildings were designed for a world that didn't have the compute to do better.

That world is over.

What Google, JLL, and Siemens Are Doing

Google deployed DeepMind AI to manage cooling in its data centres. The result: a 40% reduction in cooling energy — saving hundreds of millions of dollars annually and reducing carbon emissions from some of the world's most power-hungry infrastructure.

JLL, which manages over 4.6 billion square feet of commercial real estate globally, uses AI-powered building management across its portfolio — delivering an average 35% reduction in energy costs and cutting maintenance spend through predictive failure detection. Buildings under AI management see 20-30% fewer unplanned outages.

Siemens's Desigo CC and Johnson Controls's OpenBlue platforms use AI to continuously optimise HVAC, lighting, and access based on real-time occupancy data — adjusting every 15 minutes rather than following a fixed daily schedule.

"A building that responds to who's actually in it — not who was in it last Tuesday — is a fundamentally different infrastructure cost."

What AI Manages That Humans Can't

  • Occupancy-responsive HVAC: heating and cooling only where people actually are
  • Predictive maintenance: detecting bearing wear, motor degradation, and filter blockages weeks before failure
  • Energy benchmarking: comparing consumption against similar buildings in real time
  • Space utilisation: understanding which meeting rooms, floors, and zones are actually used — informing lease and renovation decisions

The payback period for AI-managed building systems is typically 18 to 36 months. After that, savings compound — because the AI continues learning the building's patterns and optimising as usage changes.
